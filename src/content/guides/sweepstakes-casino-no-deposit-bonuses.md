Almost every sweepstakes casino advertises a large "free" welcome bonus — often millions of Gold
Coins plus a handful of Sweeps Coins. Here's what that actually means and what to watch for.

## Why the bonuses look so large

Gold Coins have no cash value, so operators can give away large-sounding amounts (millions of
Gold Coins is a common headline figure) at effectively no cost — it's really just unlocking more
free play, not a cash giveaway. The number that actually matters is the **Sweeps Coins** portion,
since that's the prize-eligible currency. See our
[what is a Sweeps Coin](/guides/what-is-a-sweeps-coin/) guide for why that distinction matters.

## "No purchase necessary" is a real legal requirement, not just marketing

Because Sweeps Coins are redeemable for prizes, sweepstakes law requires a genuinely free way to
get them — this is exactly what makes a no-deposit welcome bonus a legal necessity for these
operators, not a generous choice. That said, some sites also advertise larger, purchase-linked
bonus bundles alongside the free option — read the terms carefully to know which offer you're
actually claiming.

## What to check before claiming one

- **Is a promo code required?** Some operators need one, some don't — and codes circulate widely
  across bonus sites, so it's worth checking the operator's own site for the current offer rather
  than trusting a code from elsewhere.
- **Is the advertised bonus actually free, or tied to a purchase?** Read the fine print; the
  headline number and the free version aren't always the same thing.
- **What's the playthrough requirement?** Bonus Sweeps Coins usually need to be played through at
  least once before you can redeem them — this varies by operator, from a light 1x requirement to
  stricter multiples.
- **Is the operator available in your state?** No bonus is worth much if you can't actually use
  it where you live — check our [legal-by-state guide](/casinos/legal-by-state/) first.
