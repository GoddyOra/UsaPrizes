This is general information, not tax advice — for anything beyond a small prize, talk to a
qualified tax professional before you do anything else. That said, here's how the basic mechanics
work.

## Federal withholding at the time you win

Under federal law (IRC §3402(q)), lottery winnings over $5,000 are subject to mandatory 24%
federal withholding, taken out automatically before you receive the prize. That withholding is
just a deposit toward your eventual tax bill — not your final tax liability.

## Why you'll likely owe more than 24%

The 24% withholding rate is well below the top federal income tax bracket (37%). A large jackpot
win pushes most or all of your taxable income for the year into that top bracket, so your actual
federal tax owed on the winnings is typically much closer to 37% than to 24%. In practice, that
means winners of large prizes usually owe a substantial additional amount when they file their
return — the gap between what was withheld and what's actually owed.

## State taxes are separate

On top of federal tax, most states also tax lottery winnings, at rates that vary significantly by
state — a handful of states don't tax lottery winnings at all, while others tax them at their
standard state income tax rate. Your state's specific rules aren't reflected in the federal
withholding taken out automatically, so factor state tax in separately when estimating what
you'll actually owe. See our full [lottery taxes by state](/guides/lottery-taxes-by-state/)
breakdown for exactly where your state falls.

## Lump sum vs. annuity

Most major lottery jackpots offer a choice between a smaller lump-sum cash payout now or the full
advertised jackpot paid out as an annuity over roughly three decades. This choice has real tax
implications — a lump sum is taxed in the year you receive it, while an annuity spreads the
taxable income (and the tax bill) across many years, which can meaningfully change your total tax
outcome. This is exactly the kind of decision worth a tax professional's input before you claim a
large prize.

## Bottom line

Don't assume the 24% withheld at the time of winning is your whole tax bill — for any meaningful
prize, it almost certainly isn't. Set aside additional funds for the remainder of your federal
liability plus state tax, and get professional advice before making any big financial decisions
with a large win.
