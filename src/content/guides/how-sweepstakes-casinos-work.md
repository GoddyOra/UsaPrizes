Sweepstakes casinos look like online casinos — slots, table games, jackpots — but they run on a
different legal structure than real-money online gambling. That structure is what makes them
available in states where traditional online casino gambling isn't legal.

## The two-currency model

Every sweepstakes casino runs on two separate currencies:

- **Gold Coins (or an equivalent branded coin)** are play-money credits with no cash value. You
  can buy bundles of them, and most sites also give some away free just for signing up. Gold
  Coins let you play the games for fun, with no prize on the other end.
- **Sweeps Coins** are the prize-eligible currency. You don't buy Sweeps Coins directly — by law,
  sweepstakes operators have to give you a way to get them for free (see below). Play through
  Sweeps Coins in the same games, and winnings can be redeemed for cash or gift cards.

Operators typically bundle a small number of free Sweeps Coins into paid Gold Coin purchases as a
promotional gift, which is legally fine as long as a genuinely free way to get them also exists.

## The "no purchase necessary" requirement

Because Sweeps Coins are redeemable for real prizes, US sweepstakes law requires every operator
to offer a free alternative method of entry (AMOE) — a way to get Sweeps Coins without spending
any money, such as a free daily login bonus or a mail-in request. This is the same legal
principle behind fast-food giveaways or contest sweepstakes: if there's a prize, there has to be
a free way to enter. It's what makes the model legally distinct from real-money gambling, where
you're wagering cash directly.

## Why this matters for legality

Real-money online casino gambling is only legal in a handful of states. Sweepstakes casinos
operate under promotional-sweepstakes law instead, which is regulated differently and legal in
most — though not all — states. Some states have moved to restrict or ban the sweepstakes model
specifically, so availability still varies by operator and by state. See our
[legal-by-state guide](/casinos/legal-by-state/) for what we've found per brand.

## Redemption: how the coins become real money

Once you've accumulated Sweeps Coins from gameplay, most operators require:

1. A minimum balance before you can request a redemption (varies by operator).
2. A playthrough requirement — you generally need to have played through your Sweeps Coins at
   least once, not just collected them.
3. Identity verification, at least the first time you redeem.

From there, payout speed depends heavily on the method: some operators offer near-instant
options (like a prepaid card or crypto), while bank transfers can take several business days. See
individual brand reviews for specifics — for example, [Chumba](/casinos/reviews/chumba/) and
[Stake.us](/casinos/reviews/stake-us/) sit at opposite ends of that range.

Sweepstakes casinos aren't a loophole or a gray area — they're built on an established legal
framework (promotional sweepstakes law) that predates online gambling entirely. Understanding the
Gold Coin / Sweeps Coin split and the no-purchase-necessary requirement is really all you need to
understand how the whole model works. From there, the differences between operators come down to
