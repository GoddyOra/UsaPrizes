Nothing dramatic happens — the jackpot simply rolls over into the next drawing, and grows. This
is exactly why headline-grabbing billion-dollar jackpots happen: a long streak of drawings with
no jackpot winner, each one adding to the pot.

## How a rollover works

Powerball and Mega Millions jackpots start at a fixed minimum after any drawing that produces a
winner, then grow with each subsequent drawing based on ticket sales, until someone finally
matches all the required numbers. Given the odds involved — roughly 1 in 290 million for either
game's jackpot, see our [odds comparison](/guides/powerball-vs-mega-millions-odds/) — it's
completely normal for a jackpot to roll over for weeks or even months in a row.

## Does the jackpot ever get "capped"?

Both games have occasionally implemented temporary caps in specific situations tied to prize-pool
mechanics, but there's no general permanent ceiling on how large a jackpot can grow through normal
rollovers. In practice, jackpots have reached well over a billion dollars multiple times for both
games.

## What happens to your ticket if the jackpot rolls over

Nothing — your specific ticket is only valid for the drawing printed on it. If you didn't win
that night, you'd need a new ticket for the next drawing to be in contention for the rolled-over
jackpot. Lower-tier prizes (matching some but not all numbers) are awarded independently of
whether the jackpot itself was won.
