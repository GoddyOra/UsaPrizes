Confirming you've won is only the first step — see our
[how to check if you won Powerball](/guides/how-to-check-powerball-numbers/) guide for that part.
Here's what actually happens next.

## First: don't rush the physical ticket

Secure the ticket somewhere safe and take a clear photo of both sides before you do anything
else. Don't sign the back immediately in every case — whether signing right away is the right
move depends on your state's specific rules and whether you're considering claiming through a
trust for anonymity (more below), so it's worth pausing on this one step specifically.

## Claim deadlines vary by state — and by payout choice

There's no single national deadline. As one concrete example, Florida gives winners 60 days to
claim a lump-sum payout but 180 days if they choose the annuity option instead — the deadline
itself depends on which payout you pick, not just which state you're in. Other states set
different windows entirely, commonly ranging from 90 days up to a year. Check your specific
state lottery's rules directly rather than assuming a number; this is exactly the kind of detail
that's easy to get wrong by guessing.

## Can you stay anonymous?

It depends entirely on where the ticket was sold — not where you live. As of 2026, roughly a
third to half of US states allow jackpot winners some form of anonymity, commonly including
Delaware, Kansas, Maryland, Missouri, Montana, New Jersey, North Dakota, South Carolina, and
Wyoming, though the exact list and the mechanism (some states allow claiming through a trust or
LLC rather than full personal anonymity) changes over time and sources don't fully agree past
that core group. See our full [state-by-state anonymity guide](/guides/lottery-winner-anonymity-by-state/)
for the complete breakdown. If anonymity matters to you, this is one of the first things worth
confirming with an attorney before you claim — not after.

## Lump sum vs. annuity

Most major jackpots offer a choice: a smaller lump-sum cash payout now, or the full advertised
jackpot paid out over roughly three decades as an annuity. This has real tax implications — see
our [lottery winnings tax guide](/guides/how-lottery-winnings-are-taxed/) — and isn't a decision
to make on the spot at the claims office. Get professional advice first. See our full
[cash option vs. annuity guide](/guides/lottery-cash-option-vs-annuity/) for how to think through
that specific tradeoff.

## Who to talk to before you claim

For any meaningful prize, the standard advice from financial professionals is consistent: line up
a tax professional and, often, an attorney *before* you formally claim, not after. Between the
lump-sum-vs-annuity decision, the anonymity question, and the actual tax bill (which is
substantially more than the 24% automatically withheld), these are decisions that are hard to
undo once made.

## The bottom line

There's no single, universal claim process — deadlines, anonymity rules, and payout options all
vary by state. Confirm your specific state lottery's process directly, and get professional
advice on the big decisions before you walk into a claims office.
