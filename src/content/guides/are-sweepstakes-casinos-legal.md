Short answer: yes, in most states — but "legal" here means something specific, and it isn't
universal. Here's the actual picture.

## The legal basis

Sweepstakes casinos don't operate under gambling law at all. They operate under state
promotional-sweepstakes law — the same general legal category that covers things like
fast-food giveaways and online contests. The key requirement is a free "alternative method of
entry" (AMOE): as long as there's a genuinely free way to get the prize-eligible currency
(Sweeps Coins), the model is legally distinct from wagering real money. See our
[how sweepstakes casinos work](/guides/how-sweepstakes-casinos-work/) guide for the mechanics.

This is why sweepstakes casinos can operate in states where real-money online casino gambling is
not licensed or regulated at all — they're not applying for a gambling license, because they're
not legally gambling operators under this framework.

## Not every state agrees

A handful of states have passed laws specifically targeting the sweepstakes-casino model,
either restricting it or prohibiting it outright, separate from their general gambling laws.
Because this list changes as state legislatures act, we don't maintain a single blanket "banned
states" list on this site — instead, our [legal-by-state guide](/casinos/legal-by-state/) tracks
what we've found per individual operator, since availability is ultimately each brand's own
compliance decision, not a single nationwide rule.

## What "legal" doesn't mean here

Legal does not mean risk-free or guaranteed-fair. It means the specific legal structure
(promotional sweepstakes, not gambling) is recognized and permitted. You should still treat it
like any other form of play: only spend what you can afford to lose on Gold Coins, understand
the redemption terms before you start, and stop if it stops being fun — free, confidential help
is available 24/7 from the National Problem Gambling Helpline (1-800-522-4700).

## Bottom line

Sweepstakes casinos are legal in most states under a well-established promotional framework, but
"most" isn't "all," and the list of restricted states isn't fixed. Always confirm current
availability for your specific state and the specific operator you're considering — our
