If Sweeps Coins are what you're playing *for*, Gold Coins (or an operator's equivalent branded
name) are what you're playing *with*. They're the other half of the two-currency model almost
every sweepstakes casino runs on.

## What a Gold Coin actually is

A Gold Coin has no cash value and can never be redeemed for a prize. It exists purely to let you
play the games — slots, table games, whatever the operator offers — for fun, the same way a
free-to-play mobile game might use in-game credits. You can typically buy bundles of Gold Coins,
and most operators also hand out a large free amount just for signing up (see our
[no-deposit bonus guide](/guides/sweepstakes-casino-no-deposit-bonuses/) for how those offers
work).

## Why they exist at all

Gold Coins are what make the "play for fun" side of a sweepstakes casino financially sustainable
for the operator without touching real-money gambling law: selling play-money credits with no
prize attached isn't gambling. The prize-eligible side — Sweeps Coins — is handled completely
separately and has to include a genuinely free way to earn them, which is the legal mechanism
that makes the whole model work. See our
[how sweepstakes casinos work](/guides/how-sweepstakes-casinos-work/) guide for the full
breakdown.

## Gold Coins vs. Sweeps Coins, quickly

| | Gold Coins | Sweeps Coins |
|---|---|---|
| Cash value | None | Redeemable for cash/gift cards |
| How you get them | Purchase, or free signup bonus | Free daily bonuses, or bundled free with a Gold Coin purchase |
| What they're for | Playing for fun only | The actual prize-eligible currency |
